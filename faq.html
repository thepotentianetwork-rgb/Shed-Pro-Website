In unincorporated Cache County, a one-story detached storage shed that is 200 square feet or less generally does not need a building permit. Sheds larger than 200 square feet, plus shops and garages, do. Even a small shed still needs zoning clearance before you place it, and setbacks, height, and lot-coverage rules still apply. If you add electrical, plumbing, or mechanical work, check with the Cache County Building Department, because that work often needs its own permit even when the shed itself is under 200 square feet. If your property is inside a city (Logan, Hyrum, Nibley, and so on), use that city’s planning office, not only the county.

If you’re unsure whether your lot is in the county or a city, Cache County’s parcel map can help. Permits and setbacks are your responsibility. ShedPro can suggest what usually applies based on what we know, but you must confirm with your city or county. ShedPro does not pull permits.

Official sources: Cache County Building FAQ · Cache County zoning clearance

In Logan, a one-story detached storage shed of 200 square feet or less generally does not need a building permit, as long as you are not adding electrical, mechanical, or plumbing. A shed over 200 square feet does need a building permit. Logan’s own shed guide also says a permit is required if you add electrical, mechanical, or plumbing, even on a smaller shed. Zoning still applies either way: setbacks, height, and how far the shed sits from the house and from the street.

Typical Logan setbacks from the city’s 2025 shed guide: a small, removable shed under 120 square feet and under 10 feet to the peak may be as close as 1 foot to the side and rear property lines; sheds over 120 square feet in residential zones are typically 8 feet from the side and 10 feet from the rear. In both cases the shed should be at least 6 feet from the house and at least 10 feet behind the front wall of the house, with larger setbacks if the side or rear yard is next to a street. Always confirm with Logan Community Development, because your lot, easements, and use can change the rule.

Official sources: Logan Shed & Detached Garage Guide (2025) · Logan Building Department

If your property is in unincorporated Box Elder County, building permits are handled by the Box Elder County Building Department. Utah’s statewide residential code — the 2021 International Residential Code — generally does not require a building permit for a one-story detached accessory structure of 200 square feet or less. That exemption does not skip zoning: you still have to meet setbacks, stay out of easements, and follow height and lot-coverage limits. Adding electrical, plumbing, or mechanical work usually needs a permit even if the shed is under 200 square feet.

The county’s public building page does not spell out the 200 square foot shed exemption in plain language the way Cache County’s FAQ does, so confirm with the Building Department before you order. Several Box Elder towns also use the county for permits, while Brigham City, Tremonton, and some others have their own offices — check which jurisdiction your address is in.

Official sources: Box Elder County Building Permits · Utah Code § 15A-2-103

Brigham City’s official accessory-building information sheet says a building permit is required if the accessory building is 201 square feet or larger, and is not required if it is 200 square feet or less. Even a shed under 200 square feet still needs zoning clearance from Community & Economic Development, and it still has to meet building-code requirements, including anchoring for wind and seismic loads. Accessory buildings are not allowed in the front yard, and they cannot sit closer to the street than the house.

Setbacks depend on your zoning district. Under the current city code, a typical accessory building is 5 feet from the rear property line (1 foot if it is 200 square feet or less and not in an easement). Side setbacks can drop to 5 feet — or 1 foot for a shed 200 square feet or less — when the building is 60 feet or more from the front property line, again if it is not in an easement. Corner lots and street-facing sides have larger setbacks. The 2016 information sheet is still posted, but the zoning numbers were updated in later ordinances, so confirm the current process and setbacks with the city (435-734-6604) before you place the shed.

Official sources: Brigham City accessory building sheet · Brigham City Code § 156.117

If your property is in unincorporated Salt Lake County, or in a Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD) member community, permits are handled by the MSD, not a separate county building office. The MSD says you do not need a building permit for a one-story detached accessory structure that is less than 200 square feet, with no plumbing, mechanical, or electrical. You do need a permit if the structure exceeds 200 square feet, and you need a permit for plumbing, mechanical, or electrical work on any size structure. The location still requires approval from Planning / Zoning (385-468-6700), even when no building permit is required.

If you are right at 200 square feet, or your lot is inside an incorporated city with its own building department (Salt Lake City, West Jordan, Sandy, and others), check with that office. Permits and setbacks are your responsibility. ShedPro can suggest what usually applies based on what we know, but you must confirm with your city or county. ShedPro does not pull permits.

Official sources: MSD: Do I Need a Building Permit? · MSD: where can I build a shed?

Salt Lake City Building Services lists residential playhouses, sheds, and similar uses under 200 square feet as a building-permit exemption. Electrical, plumbing, and HVAC work still need a permit. All zoning standards apply even when no building permit is required — setbacks, height, yard coverage, and, in a local historic district, a Certificate of Appropriateness.

Typical Salt Lake City residential placement, from the city’s October 2025 accessory-building guide: the shed is not allowed in the required front or corner yard and should sit at least as far back as the house; at least 1 foot from the side and rear lot lines (and behind the house if it is in a side yard); at least 4 feet from any part of your house, including eaves; and at least 10 feet from a house on an adjacent lot. Corner lots also need 20 feet from a public sidewalk. Confirm with Building Services (801-535-6000 or slcpermits@slc.gov), especially if you are right at 200 square feet or adding power.

Official sources: Salt Lake City Building Services FAQs · SLC accessory buildings guide (2025)

Utah County Community Development only permits unincorporated Utah County. If you are in Provo, Orem, or another city, use that city’s building office. The county officially adopts the 2021 International Residential Code, which generally exempts a one-story detached accessory structure of 200 square feet or less from a building permit. The county’s own building page does not list that size cutoff in plain language; it says some exemptions may apply and asks you to confirm with the Building Official (stevek@utahcounty.gov or 801-851-8343). Zoning still applies either way, and electrical, gas, mechanical, or plumbing work usually needs a permit.

Setbacks depend on your zone. In the RR-5 rural residential zone, buildings typically need a 30-foot front setback and 15-foot side and rear setbacks, but a residential accessory structure of 1,500 square feet or less may be 10 feet from the side and rear. Other zones differ, so check with the county before you pick a final spot.

Official sources: Utah County Building · Utah County RR-5 zone

Provo’s Building Division says accessory buildings over 200 square feet require a building permit, and accessory buildings fewer than 200 square feet do not. Setbacks and height rules still apply, and you should confirm with Zoning (801-852-6400) before you build, especially if you are right at 200 square feet or adding electrical work. Building Division: 801-852-6450.

In most Provo residential zones, a shed outside the main buildable area may be as close as 3 feet to a property line if it is more than 6 feet from the house, is not in a front or street-side yard, is no closer to the front property line than the house, stays out of utility easements, and no part of the building is more than 12 feet high within 10 feet of a property line. Always verify your zone, because some districts use different numbers.

Official sources: Provo Building Division · Provo accessory building handout

West Jordan’s official accessory-structure brochure says you need a building permit if the structure is more than 200 square feet, if it is 17 feet tall or taller, or if utility services (water, power, gas, and so on) are hooked up to it. Zoning still applies even when no permit is required, and accessory structures are not allowed in the front yard. Confirm with Planning and Zoning (801-569-5060), because agricultural zones and taller buildings have different rules.

For a structure that needs a permit, typical residential setbacks are 6 feet from the house, 3 feet from the side and rear property lines in a rear yard, 8 feet from the side line in an interior side yard, and 20 feet from the corner side property line on a corner lot (plus 3 feet from the rear). For a structure that does not need a permit, the brochure allows tighter placement: no required setback from the rear or interior side lines or from the house, 8 feet from a corner side property line, and at least 3 feet of pathway access through a side yard.

Official sources: West Jordan accessory structure brochure

Orem’s official January 2026 permit handout says a building permit is required for storage sheds exceeding 200 square feet, and is not required for one-story detached storage-type sheds 200 square feet and smaller on one- and two-family dwelling lots. Electrical, plumbing, and mechanical work still need a permit. Zoning and placement rules still apply, so confirm setbacks with Orem Community Development (801-229-7198) before you place the shed.

Official sources: Orem: When Do I Need a Building Permit? (2026)

HOA covenants can be stricter than the city or county, and those still apply. Setbacks change by city and by zoning district, so the safe move is to call your planning office with your address before you pick a final spot.

As a starting point only (always verify):

  • Logan: A small, removable shed under 120 square feet and under 10 feet to the peak may be as close as 1 foot to the side and rear; larger sheds in residential zones are typically 8 feet off the side and 10 feet off the rear, and at least 6 feet from the house and 10 feet behind the front wall of the house.
  • Unincorporated Box Elder County: In un-zoned areas, detached storage sheds are often 15 feet from the front and 5 feet from the side and rear if they stay out of a public utility easement; zoned districts can differ.
  • Brigham City: Accessory buildings are not allowed in the front yard. See question 4 for side and rear figures.
  • Unincorporated Salt Lake County / MSD: Accessory buildings generally need to be entirely in the rear yard, 6 feet from the house, and at least 1 foot from the rear and side lines, with a 14-foot height limit that can increase as you move farther from the property line, up to 20 feet.
  • Salt Lake City: Typically 1 foot from side and rear lines, 4 feet from your house (including eaves), 10 feet from a neighboring house, not in the required front or corner yard, and 20 feet from a public sidewalk on a corner lot. See question 6.
  • Unincorporated Utah County: In RR-5, typical setbacks are 30 feet in front and 15 feet on the side and rear, or 10 feet on the side and rear for a residential accessory structure of 1,500 square feet or less. Other zones differ.

  • Orem: Confirm current setbacks with Community Development; the 2026 handout covers permits, not a full setback table.
